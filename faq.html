Frequently asked questions.

Video requirements and analysis guidance.

Analysis requirements: record at 60 fps; keep the player covering at least 60% of the frame area; leave at least 5% clear space above the player’s head; keep the full body visible. 1080p is recommended, but not required for now.
  • Use a stable camera and good lighting.
  • For batting, use side-on when possible.
  • For bowling, show the full run-up, delivery stride and follow-through.

Why does video quality matter?

Analyzing a cricket video with a resolution lower than 1080p reduces the accuracy of tracking the ball, bat, and player movements.

What goes wrong

  • Ball tracking: the ball can become a blurry dot or disappear between frames.
  • Pose estimation: knee, elbow and wrist angles become less reliable.
  • Edge detection: fine bat-ball contact is difficult to verify.
  • Scoreboard blur: automated score/timer reading can fail.

How it affects analysis

  • Manual review: coaches can still watch, but fine detail is less clear.
  • AI / auto analysis: tracking may lose the ball and produce less reliable data.

Why is pace accuracy pending?

It needs ball and pitch-target tracking. CreaseIQ scores visible bowling action now but does not invent target-hit scores.
